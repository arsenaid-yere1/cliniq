# PRP Target-Area MRI Support in Initial Visit and Pain Management Reports

## Implemented follow-up (2026-09-02)

The current-state gaps documented below are addressed by the implementation plan in `thoughts/shared/plans/2026-09-02-prp-anatomic-clinical-target-validation.md`. The implemented evidence contract lives in `src/lib/clinical/prp-target-evidence.ts`, shared anatomy normalization in `src/lib/clinical/anatomic-normalization.ts`, and deterministic report rendering in `src/lib/clinical/render-prp-treatment-plan.ts`. Pain Evaluation generation, save, regeneration, target removal, and finalization enforce the contract in `src/actions/initial-visit-notes.ts`; literal Initial Visit remains PRP-free.

## Research question

Trace the current PRP target-area evaluation logic used by the Initial Visit and Pain Management reports, with particular attention to whether every recommendation establishes both (1) a documented anatomic abnormality and (2) a clinically justified treatment target. MRI is one potential source of anatomic evidence, not the complete definition of clinical target justification.

## Summary

The literal `initial_visit` report does not currently recommend PRP. Its prompt and action-layer data contract exclude MRI, CT, pain-management, and case-summary context and explicitly forbid PRP or interventional recommendations.

The PRP recommendation is produced by the `pain_evaluation_visit` branch of the same Initial Visit generator (displayed as the Pain Evaluation Visit / Pain Management Evaluation). In that branch, diagnosis selection has detailed imaging and examination support rules, but PRP target selection does not. The treatment plan is a free-text string generated by the model. The prompt asks for a bullet per target region and exact spinal levels, using cervical/lumbar regions and C4-5/C5-6/C6-7 as examples, but it does not require every emitted target to identify a documented structural abnormality or show why the patient's symptoms and examination localize that abnormality as a treatment target.

There is no deterministic abnormality-to-target concordance model or post-generation validator. The source sent to the report generator is also a synthesized case summary rather than raw diagnostic and clinical evidence. That summary can combine MRI, CT, X-ray, pain-management, orthopedic, chiropractic, and therapy sources, and its imaging entries do not retain a structured modality/source-provenance field. Consequently, the system cannot separately establish “abnormal anatomy exists here” and “the clinical presentation makes this abnormality an appropriate treatment target.” A PRP target can be introduced from symptoms alone, another report's recommendation, mixed imaging context, or model completion without both elements being established at the same region and level.

### Refined evaluation model: abnormality versus target

The clinically relevant distinction is not simply “MRI finding equals target.” The two concepts are separate:

1. **Anatomic abnormality:** a documented lesion or pathologic structure at a specific region, level, joint, or soft-tissue location. In the current data model this may originate from MRI, CT, X-ray, or another documented diagnostic source. The evidentiary value of each modality may differ by pathology.
2. **Clinically justified treatment target:** the abnormal site is plausibly responsible for the patient's presentation and appropriate for the proposed intervention. That requires concordance with region/laterality, symptom distribution, examination findings, suspected pain generator, prior treatment response, and the proposed target structure/approach.

The current Pain Evaluation logic does not encode these as two independently required gates. Its medical-necessity paragraph asks for broad correlation between examination and imaging (`src/lib/claude/generate-initial-visit.ts:331-334`), while its treatment-plan paragraph separately asks for exact PRP targets (`src/lib/claude/generate-initial-visit.ts:336-345`). Nothing requires the broad correlation statement to be demonstrated for each emitted target.

This creates two distinct unsupported-target cases:

- **No documented abnormality:** a recommended region or level is absent from the available diagnostic findings.
- **Incidental or non-concordant abnormality:** an abnormality exists, but the chart does not localize symptoms, examination findings, or the suspected pain generator to that site strongly enough to justify it as a PRP target.

The reported concern encompasses both cases. MRI-only subset validation would detect some instances of the first case, but would not establish the second.

## Detailed findings

### Report boundary

One generator supports two explicit visit types through `NoteVisitType` and `buildSystemPrompt()` in `src/lib/claude/generate-initial-visit.ts`.

- `initial_visit`: the first clinical evaluation.
- `pain_evaluation_visit`: the imaging-informed Pain Evaluation Visit, described in the prompt as a comprehensive pain management evaluation with PRP recommendations.

The shared UI exposes these as separate tabs. The Pain Evaluation Visit is the PRP-recommending report; it is not the separate telehealth Pain Management Follow-Up generator.

### Literal Initial Visit behavior

`gatherSourceData()` enables imaging context only when `visitType === 'pain_evaluation_visit'` (`src/actions/initial-visit-notes.ts:70-80`). For `initial_visit`, the case summary, prior visit, and pain-management extraction are null.

The Initial Visit prompt reinforces that boundary:

- Imaging is ordered and results are pending (`src/lib/claude/generate-initial-visit.ts:149-179`).
- Medical necessity must not recommend PRP or interventional procedures (`src/lib/claude/generate-initial-visit.ts:206-209`).
- The treatment plan must not contain a PRP protocol or cost estimate (`src/lib/claude/generate-initial-visit.ts:211-240`).
- Patient education must not mention PRP (`src/lib/claude/generate-initial-visit.ts:242-243`).

Therefore, under the current implementation, unsupported PRP targeting is not expected from the literal Initial Visit branch. If a document called an “Initial Visit report” contains PRP recommendations, the record's `visit_type`, saved draft history, and generation date should be checked to determine whether it was generated under older behavior or the Pain Evaluation branch.

### Pain Evaluation / Pain Management input data

For `pain_evaluation_visit`, `gatherSourceData()` loads:

- One approved/edited, completed `case_summaries` row containing `imaging_findings`, `suggested_diagnoses`, symptoms, and prior treatment (`src/actions/initial-visit-notes.ts:94-103`).
- The latest approved/edited pain-management extraction's diagnoses, physical examination, and diagnostic studies summary (`src/actions/initial-visit-notes.ts:105-115`, `210-226`).
- A prior finalized Initial Visit for interval comparison (`src/actions/initial-visit-notes.ts:81-92`, `228-270`).
- Only counts of approved MRI, CT, and X-ray extractions for the `hasApprovedDiagnosticExtractions` flag (`src/actions/initial-visit-notes.ts:163-180`, `208`).

The note generator does not receive raw `mri_extractions.findings`. It receives `caseSummary.imaging_findings` (`src/actions/initial-visit-notes.ts:295-303`). The entire curated input is serialized into the model request by `generateInitialVisitFromData()` (`src/lib/claude/generate-initial-visit.ts:572-606`). `curateInputDataForPrompt()` only removes empty top-level values and summarizes certain prior procedure-note text; it performs no MRI/target filtering (`src/lib/claude/context-bundle.ts`).

### Upstream imaging synthesis

The case summary is generated from all approved MRI, chiropractic, pain-management, physical-therapy, orthopedic, CT, and X-ray extractions (`src/actions/case-summaries.ts:23-108`). Its prompt explicitly incorporates pain-management treatment recommendations and combines CT and X-ray findings with MRI findings (`src/lib/claude/generate-summary.ts:33-40`).

The downstream summary imaging schema contains only:

- `body_region`
- `summary`
- `key_findings[]`
- `severity`

See `src/lib/validations/case-summary.ts:5-10` and `src/lib/claude/generate-summary.ts:61-81`. It has no structured modality, source record, MRI-only flag, or normalized spinal-level array. This loses the stronger structure available in the MRI extraction and prevents a reliable downstream set-intersection check.

### Diagnosis support is not target support

The Pain Evaluation prompt has detailed diagnostic safeguards (`src/lib/claude/generate-initial-visit.ts:291-329`):

- Diagnosis codes should correspond to actual MRI findings.
- Myelopathy requires upper-motor-neuron signs.
- Radiculopathy requires region-matched objective findings; MRI nerve-root contact alone is insufficient.
- Unsupported diagnoses are omitted or downgraded, and prose must follow the downgrade.

These rules govern diagnosis codes and related terminology. They do not define or validate PRP target regions or levels. A supported regional diagnosis also does not, by itself, identify the particular pain-generating level or structure to treat.

### PRP target selection

The operative instruction is the Pain Evaluation treatment-plan prompt (`src/lib/claude/generate-initial-visit.ts:336-345`). It asks the model to:

1. Cite MRI-confirmed pathology supporting intervention.
2. Recommend a PRP series.
3. Emit a bullet for each target region.
4. Specify exact spinal levels, guidance modality, and approach.

The examples name cervical/lumbar regions and C4-5/C5-6/C6-7. The instruction does not state that every target region and exact level must have a documented anatomic abnormality. It also does not require per-target clinical concordance with symptoms, laterality, examination findings, or a suspected pain generator; require an evidence citation per target; or prohibit copying an example region or level that is absent from the case.

This is the central gap matching the reported behavior: the model is asked to produce more specific target information than the system deterministically derives or checks. The missing evaluation is a conjunction: `documented anatomic abnormality at site` **and** `clinical evidence supports site as treatment target`.

### Comparable downstream procedure-note logic

The later PRP procedure-note generator contains a partial version of this distinction, but it does not protect Pain Evaluation recommendations upstream:

- `MULTI-LEVEL JUSTIFICATION RULE` asks for imaging/symptom concordance and prevents claiming multilevel MRI concordance when MRI supports only one treated level (`src/lib/claude/generate-procedure-note.ts:534`).
- `PRIMARY PAIN GENERATOR RULE` selects a suspected primary level from severity of anatomic pathology or objective examination concordance, and permits a diffuse/ambiguous fallback rather than fabricating a primary level (`src/lib/claude/generate-procedure-note.ts:536`).
- Non-spine multi-site prose distinguishes concordant pathology at each site from imaging concordance at only one site (`src/lib/claude/generate-procedure-note.ts:538`).
- Performed procedure sites have a nullable `target_confirmed_imaging` field, and the prompt forbids claiming imaging confirmation for a site whose flag is false or null (`src/lib/procedures/sites-helpers.ts:3-8`; `src/lib/claude/generate-procedure-note.ts:586`).

These are prompt-level narration safeguards around already selected/performed sites. They are not a deterministic clinical eligibility test, and the Pain Evaluation target recommendation has neither the structured per-site fields nor these pain-generator/concordance rules.

### Output validation and persistence

PRP targets have no structured output field. `treatment_plan` is a single string in both the Anthropic tool definition (`src/lib/claude/generate-initial-visit.ts:425-433`) and the Zod result schema (`src/lib/validations/initial-visit-note.ts:49-66`). Provider draft validation checks only that it is non-empty (`src/lib/validations/initial-visit-note.ts:72-93`).

After generation, `generateInitialVisitNote()` runs the generic `validateNarrative()` and saves the result (`src/actions/initial-visit-notes.ts:520-585`). That validator checks narrative style and consistency concerns, not MRI-to-PRP target concordance. Its warnings are non-fatal. Section regeneration reuses the same prompt and source bundle and adds no target gate (`src/lib/claude/generate-initial-visit.ts:628-688`; `src/actions/initial-visit-notes.ts:799-923`).

## Execution and data flow

1. MRI PDF is extracted into structured `mri_extractions` rows with body region and per-level findings.
2. Approved clinical extractions from multiple source types are sent to the case-summary model.
3. The model creates `case_summaries.imaging_findings` as region-level free-text synthesis without modality/source provenance.
4. Pain Evaluation generation loads that synthesized summary plus current intake/exam context, not a normalized evidence record keyed by target site.
5. The prompt asks the model to create free-text PRP target bullets with exact levels.
6. No step joins each proposed target to both an anatomic abnormality and concordant clinical findings.
7. Zod confirms only that `treatment_plan` is a string.
8. Generic narrative warnings run; no per-target abnormality/clinical-justification comparison occurs.
9. The draft is persisted and may be finalized after provider review.

## Existing tests

`src/lib/claude/__tests__/generate-initial-visit.test.ts` verifies model dispatch, tone guidance, the Initial Visit null/no-PRP contract, NSAID language, diagnosis confidence rules, MRI/exam gates for radiculopathy and myelopathy, downgrade behavior, and pain-management provenance tags.

No existing test asserts that:

- Every PRP target has a documented anatomic abnormality at the same region, level, and laterality when applicable.
- Every PRP target has concordant symptoms and/or objective examination evidence supporting it as a pain generator or treatment target.
- Every target identifies its abnormality evidence and its clinical-justification evidence separately.
- A Pain Evaluation cannot be generated when approved imaging exists but the completed case summary is absent or lacks usable MRI findings.
- The report accurately describes the modality supporting an abnormality rather than relabeling CT/X-ray or mixed-source findings as MRI confirmation.

The output-schema tests validate string shape rather than clinical target correlation.

## Open questions

1. Does “Initial Visit report” refer to a literal `visit_type='initial_visit'` record, an older generated document, or the shared Initial Visit module's Pain Evaluation tab?
2. In the observed cases, did the unsupported target originate in symptoms/current exam, a pain-management or orthopedic recommendation incorporated into the case summary, CT/X-ray findings, or model completion?
3. Was the case summary regenerated after the latest MRI provider edits? The Pain Evaluation consumes the approved completed summary rather than reading MRI rows directly.
4. Which diagnostic modalities can establish an anatomic abnormality for each proposed target type, and what minimum clinical findings make that abnormality an appropriate PRP target? The current implementation does not encode that policy.

## Verified conclusion

The reported behavior is consistent with the current code. Pain Evaluation diagnoses have imaging/exam filters, but PRP target areas are generated as unstructured prose from a mixed-source summary. The system does not require both a site-specific anatomic abnormality and site-specific clinical justification before recommending that target. The literal Initial Visit branch currently forbids PRP recommendations.
